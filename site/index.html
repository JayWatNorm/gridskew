Do Britain's power stations deliver what they plan, and does it matter for the carbon forecast?

Each unit in the Balancing Mechanism tells NESO, the grid operator, how much it plans to produce in every half-hour. NESO balances the grid by asking units to produce more or less than they planned; its instructions are public. I compare those plans with metered output from August 2025 to September 2026, using public Elexon and NESO data. I also test whether half-hours in which power stations miss their own plans are also half-hours in which NESO's carbon forecast misses by more. NESO builds its forecast from its own demand, wind and solar forecasts, not from those plans, so any link is indirect.

What I found

  • Wind strays furthest from its own plan. With no NESO instruction in force, the median wind farm missed its plan by 6.3 to 8.0% of its capacity in every whole month from September 2025 to August 2026; the median unit of every other type, by under 3.5%. Q23
  • Most under-delivery comes with no published outage. About a quarter (24%, 22 Aug 2025 – 27 Sep 2026) came with one; for wind, about a tenth. Q5
  • NESO flags almost all the energy its instructions move at wind farms as system actions, possibly for network reasons: 97% (settlement months Aug 2025 – Sep 2026). Q24
  • The carbon forecast's misses barely track under-delivery. In the fifth of half-hours with the biggest carbon misses (22 Aug 2025 – 16 Aug 2026), under-delivery was only 5% larger than in the fifth with the smallest; the test needed 25%. Over the same dates, misses in NESO's demand and wind forecasts have rank correlations of only 0.04 and 0.01 with the size of the carbon miss. Q28, Q8, Q30

The carbon forecast tested is the stored forecast (see the words below), not a day-ahead forecast. Q4 repeats the test on GridSkew's own archive of every forecast, where the timing is known.

Every reading rule was recorded before its result was read, and each result was read once (Q25 was re-run once on the same data, with the two halves of instructions that cross midnight joined); where the data had been explored first, or a result is exploratory, the details say so. "How I measured it", under each answer, gives the rule, the numbers and the dates. A figure given for scale sets a result against a context count read on 1 November 2026, a denominator, not a registered result: planned energy, planned share of capacity, national demand and carbon intensity.

Words used on this page

Plan (PN)
What a unit tells NESO it will produce, half-hour by half-hour (its Physical Notification). The latest version is used.
Instruction (BOALF)
NESO asking a unit to produce more or less than its plan, by accepting the unit's offer or bid. Elexon publishes each one.
Metered output (B1610)
What a unit actually produced in each half-hour, from Elexon's settlement data.
Settlement runs
Elexon's later recalculations of metered output with more meter data: about one week (II), three weeks (SF), two months (R1), four months (R2), seven months (R3) and 14 months (RF) after the day. Each result uses the latest run held at its read.
Under-delivery (not instructed)
How much less a unit produced than it should have: its plan, or NESO's instructed level while an instruction applied. On this page, "under-delivery" always means this.
Unit (BM unit)
A generator, battery, pumped-storage station, interconnector link or supplier's customer account in the Balancing Mechanism. Q2 covers every unit, suppliers' customer accounts included; the other questions cover units with a published fuel type: not the whole grid, and some are closed or idle.
Transmission-connected and embedded
Connected to the national transmission network, or to a local distribution network.
Fuel group, and "other"
The registry's fuel type, grouped. The "other" group is mostly batteries: units named as batteries carry 92% of its instructions.
Carbon forecast and its miss
NESO's forecast of grams of CO2 per kWh for each half-hour. The miss is NESO's estimate of the actual minus the forecast: positive means dirtier than forecast.
National demand (INDO)
NESO's initial outturn of the Great Britain generation requirement, calculated from metered generation and including transmission losses. It leaves out interconnector exports, pumped-storage pumping and power stations' own use. Wind and solar on local networks are outside that metering, so they lower it rather than count in it.
Stored forecast
The one carbon forecast NESO's API keeps for each past half-hour; NESO does not document when it is made. Where GridSkew's archive overlaps it (17 August to 10 September 2026), it matched the forecast as it stood at about the end of the half-hour; after 10 September 2026, half the stored values match none of the archive's polls.
Rank correlation
From −1 to 1; 0 means no steady rise or fall of one with the other. It ranks the values rather than using their sizes.
Mean
The total divided by the count: the everyday average.
Median
The middle value once sorted: half are above it, half below.
90th percentile
The value only 1 in 10 exceed.
Quartiles and interquartile range
The quartiles are the 25th and 75th percentiles; the interquartile range is the middle 50% of values between them.
90% range
The range holding the middle 90% of a figure's values across 1,000 resamples of whole days: a guide to uncertainty, not part of any verdict.
MW, MWh, GW, TWh, g/kWh
MW is power and MWh energy: 1 MW for a half-hour is 0.5 MWh. 1 GW is 1,000 MW; 1 TWh is a million MWh. g/kWh is grams of CO2 per kilowatt-hour.

    Do power stations stick to their plans?

    When NESO has not asked a unit to change its output, how closely does it follow its own plan? Q23

    Wind strays most. Only half-hours with no NESO instruction count. The gap shows how far a unit strayed from its plan, not why. From September 2025 to August 2026, the median wind farm missed its plan by 6.3 to 8.0% of its capacity in every whole month, against a median plan of 18 to 43% of capacity; the median unit of every other type, by under 3.5%. In September 2026, on early meter data, wind and bioenergy rose above their whole-month range; this is provisional and not explained.

    Month by month:

    How I measured it
    Measure
    Only half-hours in which no instruction applied to the unit count, so the unit was expected to follow its own plan. For each unit and month, I add up the sizes of the gaps between plan and meter and divide by what the unit could make at full registered capacity in those half-hours. I report the median unit and the 90th percentile for each fuel group and month.
    Not NESO's measure
    NESO's plan-accuracy measure also counts services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism and each unit's declared limits; this one does not, and it uses today's registered capacity. So no unit is called inaccurate, and NESO's thresholds do not apply.
    Data
    22 August 2025 to 30 September 2026, with data to 30 September 2026 22:30 UTC. Read 15 October 2026, 20:05 UTC.
    Settlement runs held
    At the read, the meter figures rest on the seven-month run (R3) from 22 August 2025 to 19 January 2026, the four-month run (R2) to 29 April, the two-month run (R1) to 5 July and for 10 to 16 August, the three-week run (SF) from 6 July to 9 August, from 17 August to 9 September and on 11 and 12 September, and the one-week run (II) on 10 September and from 13 to 30 September 2026. The three-week run for those last dates arrived after the read. Runs by month, counted on 7 November 2026, 16:59 UTC: the unit half-hour counts are as held then, not at the read.
    Left out
    Interconnectors; 18 units with no positive registered capacity (4 coal, 3 gas, 4 nuclear, 6 other, 1 wind); 237,653 half-hours of individual units with no instruction and no meter reading (3.0% of half-hours with no instruction).
    Wind cut back to relieve the network
    Over the whole window, the median wind farm missed by 7.8% of capacity on days when NESO asked at least one wind farm to cut output and flagged it as managing the network, and by 4.5% on days without (363 and 42 days): an association, not a cause.

    Does each fuel group usually produce more or less than it planned? Q3

    Slightly less, with wide spreads (22 Aug 2025 – 26 Sep 2026). Wind, gas, nuclear and bioenergy usually produced a little less than planned: wind's median is 0.3 MWh short per half-hour (about 0.6 MW), about 3.6% of a wind unit's median plan of 8.5 MWh a half-hour (mean 22.1). But a quarter of wind's half-hours fell over 2.9 MWh short and a quarter ran over 1 MWh above. Hydro, pumped storage and interconnectors have a median gap of zero: many of their half-hours show almost no gap, as when a unit planned to stay idle and did.

    Did later meter data change one week's fuel-group picture? Q22 No: no group's median gap changed sign or order, and the biggest change was 0.03 MWh per unit per half-hour (bioenergy, 10–16 Aug 2026): its median gap moved from 0.78 to 0.75 MWh. The test cannot see large corrections at single stations: in the same week, Q2 finds large revisions at three gas units between the three-week and two-month runs.

    How I measured it
    Measure

The median is signed: it shows direction, not distance. A 2 MW unit and a 1 GW unit count the same, and half-hours with an instruction are included. This is not NESO's plan-accuracy measure (see Q23).

    How much of the gap between plan and meter comes from NESO's instructions? Q12

    Most of it for gas, hydro and pumped storage (22 Aug 2025 – 26 Sep 2026): 83 to 91%, counting the instructed and the not-instructed part each by its size, and 58% for the other group. For wind (44%) and bioenergy (37%), the part NESO did not instruct is larger. NESO never instructed a nuclear unit in those dates.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    I split each half-hour's gap between plan and meter into the part NESO instructed and the part it did not. Per fuel group, I give the instructed part's size as a share of both parts' sizes added together. The parts can point opposite ways, so this is not a share of each half-hour's own gap.
    The part not instructed
    It holds services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism, which are not counted here (see Q26), and can hold wind forecast error and estimated meter readings; this split does not show which.
    Data
    The same 8,653,046 half-hours of individual units as Q3. Read 11 October 2026, with overlapping instructions ordered by time, then number; Elexon's settlement orders them by number. The two orders differ for 32 of 3,620,900 instructions.

    When units fall short, was an outage published?

    When units under-delivered with no instruction to explain it, had an outage already been published? Q5

    Mostly not. About a quarter (24%) of under-delivery (22 Aug 2025 – 27 Sep 2026) came with a published outage; for wind, the largest part, only about a tenth did. Nuclear is 39% of the under-delivery that came with a notice; without it, the share falls from 24% to 16%. Nuclear's 93% reflects standing notices of partial derating, not outages the plan missed.

    "No notice" is not "no outage": small units rarely publish. The newest 19 days, in September 2026, rest on the earliest settlement run and carry about twice the mean under-delivery per unit half-hour (3.9 MWh against 1.9); this is not explained.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    A unit's half-hour counts as "outage published" if a live outage notice for that unit, of any size, covered that half-hour when it began. These notices of reduced generating capacity are public under REMIT, the energy-market transparency rules. I add up under-delivery in those half-hours and in the rest. "Published" means published, not that the generator knew: an association, not a cause.
    Data
    8,675,654 half-hours of individual units, 22 August 2025 to 27 September 2026, with 16.4 TWh of under-delivery (about 7% of the energy these units planned over almost the same dates): planned 12.0%, unplanned 11.2%, both 0.4%, none 76.4%. Read 12 October 2026, with data to 27 September 2026 22:30 UTC.
    Left out
    255,473 half-hours of individual units with no meter reading (2.9%); 10 of 45,247 outage publications that name no unit that could be matched.

    How does NESO steer units?

    How much of the energy NESO's instructions move does it flag as system actions? Q24

    Almost all of it for wind (97% of 13.4 TWh, settlement months Aug 2025 – Sep 2026; about 14% of the energy wind farms planned over almost the same dates) and hydro (93%), and 70% for pumped storage. The flag marks an instruction as possibly for network reasons, such as relieving a bottleneck, rather than balancing supply and demand. For bioenergy, the other group (mostly batteries) and gas it is 24%, 31% and 35%.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    For every minute an instruction is in force, I add how far its instructed level is from the plan, in MWh: the energy the instruction moved. I report the share carrying NESO's system flag, which NESO sets in real time on units it uses to relieve a network bottleneck. The flag is NESO's real-time classification, not constraint volume, and later corrections to it are not read.
    Also in the data
    The level-energy share, the measure first registered: the same share on the energy at the instructed level, not its difference from the plan.
    Data
    22 August 2025 to 30 September 2026, with data to 30 September 2026 22:30 UTC. Read 15 October 2026, 20:10 UTC.

    Do batteries and pumped storage get shorter instructions than power stations? Q25

    Far more often (22 Aug 2025 – 30 Sep 2026). About one in five instructions to the other group, mostly batteries, lasts under 10 minutes (21%), and one in six to pumped storage. For gas it is about 1 in 480, and less for hydro, bioenergy and wind.

    Does any of this go with the carbon forecast's misses?

    GridSkew's own archive of every carbon forecast starts on 17 August 2026, so these four questions use the stored forecast for the year before it, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026.

    How far off is NESO's stored carbon forecast, by month and by time of day? Q29

    Its misses had a mean size of 9.3 g/kWh, about 7.5% of the mean carbon intensity of 124 g/kWh, with almost no overall bias (mean −0.6). Afternoons (12:00 to 17:59 UTC) came out about 5 g dirtier than forecast and evenings (18:00 to 23:59) about 6 g cleaner.

    In the half-hours when NESO's stored carbon forecast missed most, did units fall further short of their plans? Q28

    No. Over 22 Aug 2025 – 16 Aug 2026, the fifth of half-hours with the biggest misses carried 5% more under-delivery (90% range 3% to 8%) than the fifth with the smallest: a small but steady rise, far short of the 25% needed. Half-hours are sorted by how far the forecast missed, not which way, so this cannot say whether under-delivery makes the forecast too clean or too dirty, or by how much.

    The reverse question, how far under-delivery goes with the carbon forecast's miss, and in which direction, is registered as Q31 and Q32, read on new data in 2027.

    Exploratory: the reverse direction

    Q28 sorted by the size of the carbon miss and found a small rise in under-delivery (5%; its test needed 25%). This sorts the same half-hours by how much units under-delivered, and looks at the direction of the miss. Each extra GW of under-delivery went with a carbon miss about 2.8 g/kWh further towards dirtier than forecast (90% range 2.1 to 3.5). On average, the fifth with the least under-delivery came out 2.9 g/kWh cleaner than forecast and the fifth with the most 0.9 g/kWh dirtier; misses had a mean size of 9.3 g/kWh in either direction. Time of day and month are not held fixed, and carbon misses differ by both (Q29), so this may partly reflect them.

    Within the same time of day and month

    Within the same time of day and month, each extra GW of under-delivery went with a carbon miss 2.4 g/kWh further towards dirtier than forecast (90% range 1.8 to 3.1), against 2.8 g/kWh with neither held fixed: a positive slope remains within the same time of day and month. Holding them fixed took 0.35 g/kWh per GW off the slope (90% range 0.05 to 0.67). The 90% range is above zero in all four time-of-day blocks and in 7 of the 13 months; in the other 6 it includes zero, and the slope itself is at or below zero only in December, January and February.

    When NESO's stored carbon forecast misses, does a demand-forecast miss or under-delivery go with it more? Q8

    No clear difference (22 Aug 2025 – 16 Aug 2026): ranking half-hours by the size of each miss and by under-delivery, both links are very weak, with rank correlations of 0.04 for demand and 0.05 for under-delivery. The gap between them is −0.01 (90% range −0.04 to 0.02); to count as stronger, one link needed to lead by 0.1.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    For each half-hour I rank how far the stored carbon forecast missed, how far the last demand forecast missed, and total under-delivery. One link counts as stronger only if its rank correlation beats the other by 0.1 or more. The demand miss is the first Initial National Demand Outturn (INDO) minus NESO's last national demand forecast (NDF) before the half-hour.
    Data
    The same 17,278 half-hours as Q28, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026. Read 26 October 2026, 11:21 UTC.

    When NESO's wind forecast misses, does its stored carbon forecast miss too? Q30

    Barely: over 22 Aug 2025 – 16 Aug 2026, the sizes of the two misses have a rank correlation of 0.01, inside the ±0.2 written down as expected before the read. The direction, also set beforehand, came out slightly the wrong way (+0.07, expected negative); on delivered wind alone it is −0.17, which may reflect wind NESO turned down; that is not tested here.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    For each half-hour I compare metered wind, with wind NESO turned down added back, against the latest wind forecast published before it (WINDFOR, issued up to eight times a day), using its hourly point without interpolation or fall-back. A wind miss is outturn minus forecast, so positive means more wind than forecast, which was expected to go with a cleaner-than-forecast carbon outcome (a negative correlation). I measure how closely the sizes and the directions of the two misses rank together; there is no test.
    Data
    17,280 half-hours on 360 days, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026. Forecast ages from the issue time on each forecast: up to 1 hour for 5,756 half-hours, 1 to 2 hours for 5,756, 2 to 4 hours for 5,766 and over 4 hours for 2. The carbon forecast is the stored one, set against a wind forecast up to hours old. Metered wind only, with no embedded wind; an association, not a cause. Read 27 October 2026, on data captured up to 14:03:55 UTC.

    Can the metered data be trusted?

    How much do metered figures change when Elexon recalculates settlement with later meter data? Q2

    Materially: for settlement dates 10–16 Aug 2026, 3.4% of the energy was revised between the one-week and three-week runs, and 2.3% between the three-week and two-month runs, 77% of that in suppliers' customer accounts. Between the three-week and two-month runs, transmission-connected and embedded units changed in 0.7% of half-hours, but three gas units' revisions, up or down counted alike, came to about 40 GWh, 35% of their own 114 GWh in the three-week run, and 18% of the energy revised between the three-week and two-month runs.

    How I measured it
    Measure
    For 10 to 16 August 2026 I compared every unit's half-hourly figure in the runs made about one week, three weeks and two months after each day. The energy revised is the sum of every half-hour's change, up or down counted alike, as a share of the earlier run's total (9,348 GWh for the one-week run, 9,494 GWh for the three-week run). A change of 2% or more of the energy, or in 20% or more of half-hours, counts as material.
    Data
    3,083,376 matched half-hourly figures in each pair, one per unit per half-hour, none in one run only. Every unit counts, suppliers' customer accounts included. Read 11 October 2026.
    The three gas units
    At two of them, the three-week run filled two days the one-week run had at zero, and the two-month run zeroed one of them. At the third, the three-week run repeated the one-week values, and the two-month run replaced every value from 11 to 16 August. Each unit's revised energy, net change and three-week-run energy are in a saved output; the units are lettered, not named.
    Rule
    Recorded 25 September 2026, before any metered figure was read. It tests only the two run-pair shares; the split by unit type and the three gas units are described after the read.     Limitations

    • Fuel groups are the current registry's.
    • The metered figure is the latest settlement run held at each read, which differs by date.
    • The plan (PN) is the latest version captured, not the position an hour before delivery, when plans become final.
    • Services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism are not in the instructions (BOALF).
    • The units studied are not the whole grid: wind and solar on local networks outside the Balancing Mechanism, and batteries with no published fuel type, are not in them.
    • Results that set one measure against another (Q5, Q8, Q28, Q30 and the exploratory cuts) are associations, not causes. Each result covers the period given beside it and may not hold in another.
