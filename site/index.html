Do Britain's power stations deliver what they plan, and does it matter for the carbon forecast?

Each unit in the Balancing Mechanism tells NESO, the grid operator, how much it plans to produce in every half-hour. NESO balances the grid by asking units to produce more or less than they planned; its instructions are public. We compare those plans with metered output from August 2025 to September 2026, using public Elexon and NESO data. We also test whether half-hours in which power stations miss their own plans are also half-hours in which NESO's carbon forecast misses by more. NESO builds its forecast from its own demand, wind and solar forecasts, not from those plans, so any link is indirect.

What we found

  • Wind strays furthest from its own plan. With no NESO instruction in force, the median wind farm missed its plan by more than the median unit of any other type in every whole month from September 2025 to August 2026. Q23
  • Most under-delivery comes with no published outage. About a quarter (24%) came with one; for wind, about a tenth. Q5
  • NESO flags almost all the energy its instructions move at wind farms as managing the network: 97%. Q24
  • The carbon forecast's misses barely track any of it. In the fifth of half-hours with the biggest carbon misses, under-delivery was only 5% larger than in the fifth with the smallest; the test needed 25%. Misses in NESO's demand and wind forecasts show only weak links too. Q28, Q8, Q30

The carbon forecast tested is the stored forecast (see the words below), not a day-ahead forecast. Q4 repeats the test on GridSkew's own archive of every forecast, where the timing is known.

Every reading rule was recorded before its result was read, and each result was read once; where the data had been explored first, the details say so. "How we measured it", under each answer, gives the rule, the numbers and the dates.

Words used on this page

Plan (PN)
What a unit tells NESO it will produce, half-hour by half-hour (its Physical Notification). We use the latest version.
Instruction (BOALF)
NESO asking a unit to produce more or less than its plan, by accepting the unit's offer or bid. Elexon publishes each one.
Metered output (B1610)
What a unit actually produced in each half-hour, from Elexon's settlement data.
Settlement runs
Elexon's later recalculations of metered output with more meter data: about one week, three weeks, two months, five months, seven months and 14 months after the day. We use the latest run held when we read.
Under-delivery not instructed
How much less a unit produced than it should have: its plan, or NESO's instructed level while an instruction applied.
Unit (BM unit)
A generator, battery, pumped-storage station, interconnector link or supplier's customer account in the Balancing Mechanism. Q2 covers every unit, suppliers' customer accounts included; the other questions cover units with a published fuel type: not the whole grid, and some are closed or idle.
Fuel group, and "other"
The registry's fuel type, grouped. The "other" group is mostly batteries: units named as batteries carry 92% of its instructions.
Carbon forecast and its miss
NESO's forecast of grams of CO2 per kWh for each half-hour. The miss is NESO's estimate of the actual minus the forecast: positive means dirtier than forecast.
Stored forecast
The one carbon forecast NESO's API keeps for each past half-hour; NESO does not document when it is made. Where GridSkew's archive overlaps it (17 August to 10 September 2026), it matched the forecast as it stood at about the end of the half-hour; after 10 September 2026, half the stored values match none of the archive's polls.
Rank correlation
From −1 to 1; 0 means no link. It ranks the values rather than using their sizes.
Mean
The total divided by the count: the everyday average.
Median
The middle value once sorted: half are above it, half below.
90th percentile
The value only 1 in 10 exceed.
Interquartile range
The middle 50% of values, from the 25th to the 75th percentile.
90% range
Where a figure falls in 90% of 1,000 resamples of whole days: a guide to uncertainty, not part of any verdict.

    Do power stations stick to their plans?

    When NESO has not asked a unit to change its output, how closely does it follow its own plan? Q23

    Wind strays most. Only half-hours with no NESO instruction count, so any gap is the unit's own miss. From September 2025 to August 2026, the median wind farm missed its plan by 6.3 to 8.0% of its capacity in every whole month; the median unit of every other type, by under 3.5%. In September 2026, on early meter data, wind and bioenergy rose above their whole-month range; this is provisional and not explained.

    Month by month:

    How we measured it
    Measure
    Only half-hours in which no instruction applied to the unit count, so the unit was expected to follow its own plan. For each unit and month, we add up the sizes of the gaps between plan and meter and divide by what the unit could make at full registered capacity in those half-hours. We report the median unit and the 90th percentile for each fuel group and month.
    Not NESO's measure
    NESO's plan-accuracy measure also counts services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism and each unit's declared limits; this one does not, and it uses today's registered capacity. So no unit is called inaccurate, and NESO's thresholds do not apply.
    Data
    22 August 2025 to 30 September 2026, with data to 30 September 2026 22:30 UTC. Read 15 October 2026, 20:05 UTC.
    Left out
    Interconnectors; 18 units with no positive registered capacity (4 coal, 3 gas, 4 nuclear, 6 other, 1 wind); 237,653 half-hours of individual units with no instruction and no meter reading. Counted again on 31 October 2026, half-hours without a meter reading are about 3% of the total: 4.4% to 5.0% a month from November 2025 to March 2026 and 1.7% in September 2026, mostly in the other group (14.5% of its half-hours).
    Wind cut back to relieve the network
    Over the whole window, the median wind farm missed by 7.8% of capacity on days when NESO asked at least one wind farm to cut output and flagged it as managing the network, and by 4.5% on days without (363 and 42 days): an association, not a cause.

    Does each fuel group usually produce more or less than it planned? Q3

    Slightly less, with wide spreads. Wind, gas, nuclear and bioenergy usually produced a little less than planned: wind's median is 0.3 MWh short per half-hour (about 0.6 MW). But a quarter of wind's half-hours fell over 2.9 MWh short and a quarter ran over 1 MWh above. Hydro, pumped storage and interconnectors have a median gap of zero: many of their half-hours show almost no gap, as when a unit planned to stay idle and did.

    Did later meter data change one week's fuel-group picture? Q22 No: no group's median gap changed sign or order, and the biggest change was 0.03 MWh per unit per half-hour (bioenergy). The test cannot see large corrections at single stations.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For every unit and half-hour with both a plan and a meter reading, we take plan minus meter, so positive means it produced less. We report the median and the quartiles for each fuel group. The median is signed: it shows direction, not distance. A 2 MW unit and a 1 GW unit count the same, and half-hours with an instruction are included. This is not NESO's plan-accuracy measure (see Q23).
    Data
    8,653,046 half-hours of individual units, 22 August 2025 to 26 September 2026. The meter figure is the latest settlement run held at the read, which moves with the date. Read 11 October 2026.
    Left out
    583,913 half-hours of individual units with a plan and no meter reading, and 283,242 with a meter reading and no plan.

    How much of the gap between plan and meter comes from NESO's instructions? Q12

    Most of it for gas, hydro and pumped storage: 83 to 91%, counting the instructed and the not-instructed part each by its size. For wind, instructions are 44%, so the part NESO did not instruct is larger. NESO never instructed a nuclear unit in this period.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    We split each half-hour's gap between plan and meter into the part NESO instructed and the part it did not. Per fuel group, we give the instructed part's size as a share of both parts' sizes added together. The parts can point opposite ways, so this is not a share of each half-hour's own gap.
    The part not instructed
    It includes services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism, wind forecast error and settlement estimates.
    Data
    The same 8,653,046 half-hours of individual units as Q3. Read 11 October 2026, with overlapping instructions ordered by time, then number; Elexon's settlement orders them by number. The two orders differ for 32 of 3,620,900 instructions.

    When units fall short, was an outage published?

    When units under-delivered against their plan, had an outage already been published? Q5

    Mostly not. About a quarter (24%) of under-delivery came with a published outage; for wind, the largest part, only about a tenth did. Nuclear's 93% reflects standing notices of partial derating, not outages the plan missed.

    "No notice" is not "no outage": small units rarely publish. The newest 19 days, in September 2026, carry about twice the mean under-delivery per half-hour; this is not explained.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    A unit's half-hour counts as "outage published" if a live outage notice for that unit, of any size, covered that half-hour when it began. These notices of reduced generating capacity are public under REMIT, the energy-market transparency rules. We add up under-delivery in those half-hours and in the rest. "Published" means published, not that the generator knew: an association, not a cause.
    Data
    8,675,654 half-hours of individual units, 22 August 2025 to 27 September 2026, with 16.4 TWh of under-delivery: planned 12.0%, unplanned 11.2%, both 0.4%, none 76.4%. Read 12 October 2026, with data to 27 September 2026 22:30 UTC.
    Left out
    255,473 half-hours of individual units with no meter reading; 10 notices that name no unit we can match.
    Rule
    Recorded 12 October 2026, before any value was read. Full rule · query q5_shortfall_with_known_outage.sql · saved output · chart data

    How soon after the start time written in an outage notice does it appear? Q6

    The median unplanned notice appears about a minute after its written start, and 9 in 10 within 30 minutes. About two in three (7,541 of 11,811) give a start exactly on the hour or half-hour, so this is not the time from failure to notice.

    How does NESO steer units?

    How much of the energy NESO's instructions move does it flag as managing the network? Q24

    Almost all of it for wind (97% of 13.4 TWh) and hydro (93%), and 70% for pumped storage. The flag marks an instruction as possibly for network reasons, such as relieving a bottleneck, rather than balancing supply and demand. For bioenergy, the other group (mostly batteries) and gas it is 24%, 31% and 35%.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For every minute an instruction is in force, we add how far its instructed level is from the plan, in MWh: the energy the instruction moved. We report the share carrying NESO's system flag, which NESO sets in real time on units it uses to relieve a network bottleneck. The flag is NESO's real-time classification, not constraint volume, and later corrections to it are not read.
    Also in the data
    The level-energy share, the measure first registered: the same share on the energy at the instructed level, not its difference from the plan.
    Data
    22 August 2025 to 30 September 2026, with data to 30 September 2026 22:30 UTC. Read 15 October 2026, 20:10 UTC.
    Rule

    Do batteries and pumped storage get shorter instructions than power stations? Q25

    Far more often. About one in five instructions to the other group, mostly batteries, lasts under 10 minutes (21%), and one in six to pumped storage. For gas it is about 1 in 480, and less for hydro, bioenergy and wind.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    We join each instruction to the unit's instructions that overlap or touch it, issued within three half-hours either side. We count instructions whose joined run lasts under 10 minutes, Elexon's own test, which our count matched on all 20,680 checked (21 to 27 September 2026).

Battery units with no published fuel type are not in the other group.

    Does any of this go with the carbon forecast's misses?

    GridSkew's own archive of every carbon forecast starts on 17 August 2026, so these four questions use the stored forecast for the year before it, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026.

    How far off is NESO's stored carbon forecast, by month and by time of day? Q29

    Its misses had a mean size of 9.3 g/kWh, with almost no overall bias (mean −0.6). Afternoons (12:00 to 17:59 UTC) came out about 5 g dirtier than forecast and evenings (18:00 to 23:59) about 6 g cleaner.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For every half-hour from 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026, we take NESO's estimate of what happened minus the forecast its API kept (positive = dirtier than forecast). We describe it by month and by six-hour block of the day in UTC; there is no test.
    Data
    17,280 half-hours, each with an error. Winter months came out cleaner than forecast (down to −3.4 in November 2025), April to July slightly dirtier; 1 to 16 August 2026 had the largest misses (mean size 12.1 g, 16 days). The overall mean and median were seen in a September 2026 exploration, measured with the opposite sign. The "actual" is NESO's own estimate; each month is seen once; this is not a judgement of NESO's method. Read 27 October 2026, 06:15 UTC.
    Coverage
    GridSkew's data hold an impossible 0 g/kWh actual for 6 August 2026, 11:00 UTC, which falls in the August 2026 cell.

    When NESO's stored carbon forecast misses by more, do units fall further short of their plans? Q28

    No. The fifth of half-hours with the biggest misses carried 5% more under-delivery (90% range 3% to 8%) than the fifth with the smallest: a small but steady rise, far short of the 25% needed.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    Each half-hour, we add up every studied unit's under-delivery not instructed; one unit's over-delivery does not cancel another's. "Yes" if the fifth of half-hours with the biggest carbon misses carries at least 25% more under-delivery than the fifth with the smallest. NESO's forecast does not use units' plans, and this measure does not weigh fuels by carbon.
    Data
    17,278 half-hours on 360 days, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026; two half-hours on one day have no under-delivery figure. Misses are whole grams, so neighbouring fifths share an edge: 1,360 half-hours missed by exactly 3 g. The overall error of this forecast had already been seen in a September 2026 exploration. Read 26 October 2026, 11:21 UTC, with Q8.
    Settlement runs held
    The seven-month run (R3) from 22 August 2025 to 19 January 2026, the five-month run (R2) to 29 April, the two-month run (R1) to 5 July and for 10 to 16 August, and the three-week run (SF) from 6 July to 9 August 2026.
    Coverage
    GridSkew's data hold an impossible 0 g/kWh actual for 6 August 2026, 11:00 UTC, which also falls in Q29's August 2026 cell.

    When NESO's stored carbon forecast misses, does a demand-forecast miss or under-delivery go with it more? Q8

    No clear difference: both links are very weak, with rank correlations of 0.04 for demand and 0.05 for under-delivery. The gap between them is −0.01 (90% range −0.04 to 0.02); to count as stronger, one link needed to lead by 0.1.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For each half-hour we rank how far the stored carbon forecast missed, how far the last demand forecast missed, and total under-delivery. One link counts as stronger only if its rank correlation beats the other by 0.1 or more. The demand miss is the first Initial National Demand Outturn (INDO) minus NESO's last national demand forecast (NDF) before the half-hour.
    Data
    The same 17,278 half-hours as Q28. Read 26 October 2026, 11:21 UTC.

    When NESO's wind forecast misses, does its carbon forecast miss too? Q30

    Barely: the sizes of the two misses have a rank correlation of 0.01, inside the ±0.2 written down as expected before the read. The direction, also set beforehand, came out slightly the wrong way (+0.07, expected negative); on delivered wind alone it is −0.17, which may reflect wind NESO turned down and needs its own test.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For each half-hour we compare metered wind, with wind NESO turned down added back, against the latest wind forecast published before it (WINDFOR, issued up to eight times a day), using its hourly point without interpolation or fall-back. We measure how closely the sizes and the directions of the two misses rank together; there is no test.
    Data
    17,280 half-hours on 360 days, 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026. Forecast ages from the source stamp: up to 1 hour for 5,756 half-hours, 1 to 2 hours for 5,756, 2 to 4 hours for 5,766 and over 4 hours for 2. Metered wind only, with no embedded wind; an association, not a cause. Read 27 October 2026, on data captured up to 14:03:55 UTC.
    Coverage
    21 first wind outturns were published more than 30 minutes late, 3 of them zero; those over an hour late or zero all fall on six dates (27 November 2025; 15 January, 24 February, 4 June, 7 July and 6 August 2026). GridSkew's data also hold an impossible 0 g/kWh carbon actual for 6 August 2026, 11:00 UTC, which falls in Q29's August 2026 cell.

    Can the metered data be trusted?

    How much do metered figures change when Elexon recalculates settlement with later meter data? Q2

    Materially: 3.4% of the energy was revised between the one-week and three-week runs, and 2.3% between the three-week and two-month runs, 77% of that in suppliers' customer accounts. Transmission-connected and embedded units changed in under 1% of half-hours, but three gas units' figures changed by thousands of MWh.

    How we measured it
    Measure
    For 10 to 16 August 2026 we compared every unit's half-hourly figure in the runs made about one week, three weeks and two months after each day. A change of 2% or more of the energy, or in 20% or more of half-hours, counts as material.
    Data
    3,083,376 matched half-hourly figures in each pair, one per unit per half-hour, none in one run only. Every unit counts, suppliers' customer accounts included. Read 11 October 2026.
    The three gas units
    At two of them, the three-week run filled two days the one-week run had at zero, and the two-month run zeroed one of them. At the third, the three-week run repeated the one-week values, and the two-month run replaced every value from 11 to 16 August.

    Limitations

    • Fuel groups are the current registry's.
    • The metered figure is the latest settlement run held at each read, which differs by date.
    • The plan (PN) is the latest version captured, not the position an hour before delivery, when plans become final.
    • Services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism are not in the instructions (BOALF).
    • The units studied are not the whole grid.
