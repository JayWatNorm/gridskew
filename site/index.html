Do Britain's power stations deliver what they plan, and does it matter for the carbon forecast?

Each unit in the Balancing Mechanism tells NESO, the grid operator, how much it plans to produce in every half-hour. NESO balances the grid by asking units to produce more or less than they planned; its instructions are public. I compare those plans with metered output from August 2025 to September 2026, using public Elexon and NESO data. I also look at whether half-hours in which units miss their own plans are also half-hours in which NESO's carbon forecast misses by more. NESO builds its forecast from its own demand, wind and solar forecasts, not from those plans, so any link is indirect.

What I found

  • Wind strays furthest from its own plan. With no NESO instruction in force, the median wind farm missed its plan by 6.3 to 8.0% of its capacity in every whole month from September 2025 to August 2026; the median unit of every other type, by under 3.5%.*
  • Most under-delivery comes with no published outage. About a quarter (24%, 22 Aug 2025 – 27 Sep 2026) came with one; for wind, about a tenth.*
  • NESO flags almost all the energy its instructions move at wind farms as system actions, possibly for network reasons: 97% (settlement months Aug 2025 – Sep 2026).*
  • The carbon forecast's misses only slightly track under-delivery, and barely track misses in NESO's demand and wind forecasts. In the fifth of half-hours with the biggest carbon misses (22 Aug 2025 – 16 Aug 2026), under-delivery was only 5% larger than in the fifth with the smallest (90% range 3% to 8%).* Over the same dates, misses in NESO's demand and wind forecasts have rank correlations of only 0.04* and 0.01* with the size of the carbon miss.
  • After 10 September 2026, up to 12 November, the carbon forecast was revised upward as each half-hour approached. Over the whole window from 19 August 2026 (2,910 half-hours) the middle change from first to final forecast was +19 g/kWh,* and a day ahead the forecast ran 21 g/kWh below NESO's outturn estimate (2,864 half-hours); the accuracy figures point the other way on the 365 earliest half-hours (19 August to 4 September 2026).*

The carbon forecast set against under-delivery, demand and wind is the stored forecast (see the words below), not a day-ahead forecast. No result sets under-delivery, demand or wind against the forecasts in GridSkew's own archive, where the timing is known.

"How I measured it", under each answer, gives the measure, the numbers and the dates.

Words used on this page

Plan (PN)
What a unit tells NESO it will produce, half-hour by half-hour (its Physical Notification). The latest version is used.
Instruction (BOALF)
NESO asking a unit to produce more or less than its plan, by accepting the unit's offer or bid. Elexon publishes each one.
Metered output (B1610)
What a unit actually produced in each half-hour, from Elexon's settlement data.
Settlement runs
Elexon's later recalculations of metered output with more meter data: about one week (II), three weeks (SF), two months (R1), four months (R2), seven months (R3) and 14 months (RF) after the day for the days studied here; for days from 1 October 2026 the final run comes at about seven months. Each result uses the latest run held at its read.
Under-delivery (not instructed)
How much less a unit exported, or how much more it imported, than expected: its plan, or NESO's instructed level while an instruction applied. For a generating unit, this usually means it produced less than it should have. On this page, "under-delivery" always means this.
BM unit (unit)
A generator, battery, pumped-storage station, interconnector link or supplier's customer account in the Balancing Mechanism. The answer on settlement revisions* covers every unit, suppliers' customer accounts included; what is turned up with wind turned down* covers every unit NESO instructed; the other questions cover units with a published fuel type: not the whole grid, and some are closed or idle. Some of those are registered by suppliers or virtual lead parties: about 5% of the half-hours behind more or less than planned*, the instructed share* and published outages*, and 4% behind following the plan*. Their metered figures are settlement allocations, not a station's meter (unit types at each read).
Transmission-connected and embedded
Connected to the national transmission network, or to a local distribution network.
Fuel group, and "other"
The registry's fuel type, grouped, as it stood at each read. The "other" group is mostly batteries: units identified as batteries by name or ID carry 92% of its instructions. Since the read of 15 October 2026 on following the plan*, the registry has given 24 more units a fuel type (15 other, 8 gas, 1 bioenergy) and changed none. Counted on 7 November 2026, that read's half-hours would include 344,950 more at 23 of those units (4.7%), and 801 more at the read's own units, whose first meter figures arrived after the read (cohort change).
Carbon forecast and its miss
NESO's forecast of grams of CO2 per kWh for each half-hour. The miss is NESO's estimate of the actual minus the forecast: positive means dirtier than forecast.
National demand (INDO)
NESO's initial outturn of the Great Britain generation requirement, calculated from metered generation and including transmission losses. It leaves out interconnector exports, pumped-storage pumping and power stations' own use. Wind and solar on local networks are outside that metering, so they lower it rather than count in it.
Stored forecast
The one carbon forecast NESO's API keeps for each past half-hour; NESO does not document when it is made. Where GridSkew's archive overlaps it (17 August to 10 September 2026), it matched a late poll of the archive, in a check whose output is not published; after 10 September 2026, half the stored values match none of the archive's polls.
Rank correlation
From −1 to 1; 0 means no steady rise or fall of one with the other. It ranks the values rather than using their sizes.
Mean
The total divided by the count: the everyday average.
Median
The middle value once sorted: half are above it, half below.
90th percentile
The value only 1 in 10 exceed.
Quartiles and interquartile range
The quartiles are the 25th and 75th percentiles; the interquartile range is the middle 50% of values between them.
90% range
The range holding the middle 90% of a figure's values across 1,000 resamples of whole days: a guide to how firm a figure is.
MW, MWh, GW, TWh, g/kWh
MW is power and MWh energy: 1 MW for a half-hour is 0.5 MWh. 1 GW is 1,000 MW; 1 TWh is a million MWh. g/kWh is grams of CO2 per kilowatt-hour.

How I measured it
Result

Does each fuel group usually produce more or less than it planned?

Below plan in the typical half-hour, for most groups. Added up over the whole period, the totals can lean the other way: the energy metered at gas units came to about 19% more than planned, while wind units came to 15% less and hydro units 19% less. Nuclear and bioenergy units each came to about 3% less.

These totals include half-hours when NESO told units to change output, such as asking wind farms to cut back; instructions make up most of gas's and hydro's gaps* and none of nuclear's. So each percentage mixes NESO's instructions with units straying from their own plans.

Did later meter data change one week's fuel-group picture? No. For 10–16 Aug 2026, no group's median gap changed sign or order, and the biggest change was 0.03 MWh per unit per half-hour. This comparison cannot see large corrections at single stations.*

The typical half-hour: medians and spreads

Below plan in the typical half-hour, for most groups. At wind, gas, nuclear, bioenergy and other units the median half-hour fell short of plan; for hydro, pumped storage and interconnectors the median gap is zero. Wind's spread is wide: a quarter of its half-hours fell over 2.9 MWh short and a quarter ran over 1 MWh above.

The median shows which way the typical half-hour leans, not how much energy a group gained or lost, and a 2 MW unit counts the same as a 1 GW unit.

Measure
For every unit and half-hour with both a plan and a meter reading, I take plan minus meter, so positive means it produced less. I report the median and the quartiles for each fuel group. The median is signed: it shows direction, not distance. A 2 MW unit and a 1 GW unit count the same, and half-hours with an instruction are included. This is not NESO's plan-accuracy measure (see Q23).
Energy against plan
Context from the same saved output, not the measure set in advance. For each group I add up the size of every unit's metered energy, half-hour by half-hour, and the size of its planned energy, over the same half-hours as the medians, and give the first as a percentage above or below the second, to the nearest whole percent: wind 80.5 against 94.9 TWh, gas 77.6 against 65.4, nuclear 35.3 against 36.5, bioenergy 21.8 against 22.3, hydro 2.6 against 3.2. Sizes are added whatever their direction, so power a unit draws while idle counts as energy produced. The outage answer*, whose half-hours run one day longer, counts 0.8 TWh drawn by gas units when nothing was expected of them; taken off rather than added, gas comes to about 16% above plan, not 19%, and nuclear about 4% below, not 3%. A group's gap is its instructed part plus the rest, and the rest can be no larger than its summed size in the instructed-share answer*: so instructions account for more than half of gas's excess and at least three-quarters of hydro's shortfall, and for wind the same sums leave it open, from none of the shortfall to nine-tenths of it. Pumped storage, the other group (mostly batteries) and interconnectors take power in as well as send it out, so for them a ratio of sizes does not show more or less produced; they are left out, as is coal, which has no plan.

How much of the gap between plan and meter comes from NESO's instructions?

Most of it for gas, hydro and pumped storage: 83 to 91%, and 58% for the other group. For wind (44%) and bioenergy (37%), the part NESO did not instruct is larger.

The part not instructed is not all the unit's doing: it holds services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism, and can hold wind forecast error and estimated meter readings.

Measure

How does NESO steer units?

How much of the energy NESO's instructions move does it flag as system actions?

Almost all of it for wind: 97%. The flag marks an instruction as possibly for network reasons, such as relieving a bottleneck, rather than balancing supply and demand. Hydro 93%, pumped storage 70%, gas 35%, the other group (mostly batteries) 31%, bioenergy 24%.

The flag is NESO's label in real time, not a measured volume of network constraint, and later corrections to it are not read.

How I measured it
Result
Almost all of it for wind (97% of 13.4 TWh, settlement months Aug 2025 – Sep 2026; about 14% of the energy wind farms planned over almost the same dates) and hydro (93%), and 70% for pumped storage. For bioenergy, the other group (mostly batteries) and gas it is 24%, 31% and 35%.
Chart
Settlement months Aug 2025 – Sep 2026; the first is a part month, from 22 August 2025. Units with a published fuel type only: in the count of short instructions* they carry 42.5% of all instructions by number (1,495,345 of 3,516,590). Coal and nuclear units had none.
Measure
For every minute an instruction is in force, I add how far its instructed level is from the plan, in MWh: the energy the instruction moved. I report the share carrying NESO's system flag, which marks an instruction as a system action, possibly for network reasons. The flag is NESO's real-time classification, not constraint volume, and later corrections to it are not read. For the minutes when a later instruction replaces an earlier one, the full difference between the later instructed level and the plan uses the later instruction's flag.

When NESO turns wind farms down, what does it turn up?

Mostly gas, and on average more gas the more wind is turned down in the same half-hour. NESO turned wind down as a system action in 66% of half-hours, and by 1 GW or more in 45%. Where wind is turned down as a system action, gas is 85% of the energy turned up, 2.4 GW on average; elsewhere, gas is still 90% of it, but 0.8 GW.

This shows what NESO did in the same half-hours, not that one instruction replaced another; no location and no price is read.

How I measured it
Result
NESO turned down 14.9 TWh at wind farms as system actions (settlement months Aug 2025 – Oct 2026), 14% of the energy the wind farms planned; by month from 9% (August 2026) to 22% (September 2026). Of 20,928 half-hours, 13,873 have such a turn-down (66%), 12,580 one of 100 MW or more (60%) and 9,322 one of 1 GW or more (45%). In the 13,873, NESO turned up 19.4 TWh, 16.4 TWh of it at gas units (85%). In the 7,055 with none, it turned up 3.1 TWh, 2.8 TWh of it at gas units (90%). By band, gas averages 0.8 GW with no wind turned down as a system action and 3.3 GW at 2 GW or more, while its share of the energy turned up falls from 90% to 84%.
Chart
Settlement months Aug 2025 – Oct 2026; the first is a part month, from 22 August 2025. Each half-hour is placed by the wind NESO turned down in it as a system action; a half-hour's energy in MWh times two is its average power in MW. Everything else is bioenergy, hydro, pumped storage, wind, the other group and units with no published fuel type.
Measure
For every minute an instruction is in force, I take how far its instructed level is from the plan, in MWh: turned down where it is below the plan, turned up where it is above. Wind turned down counts where the instruction carries NESO's system flag; energy turned up counts with or without it, because energy turned up to replace energy turned down behind a bottleneck need not carry the flag (28% of the gas energy turned up in half-hours with wind turned down as a system action does).
Left out
Nothing: every instruction in the period had a plan to compare with, a single ramp to read its level from and a flag value.
Limits
Only Balancing Mechanism instructions are read: no trades, and no wind outside the Balancing Mechanism. At a battery, pumped-storage, interconnector or demand unit, turned up can mean importing less. Half-hours with wind turned down differ from the others in wind and demand, and nothing here allows for that. Gas is most of the energy NESO turns up in every band, including half-hours with no wind turned down as a system action. For the minutes when a later instruction replaces an earlier one, the full difference between the later instructed level and the plan uses the later instruction's flag. This can affect which wind turn-down band the half-hour falls into.

Do batteries and pumped storage get shorter instructions than power stations?

Yes, far more often. About one in five instructions to the other group, mostly batteries, lasts under 10 minutes (21%), and one in six to pumped storage. For gas it is about 1 in 480, and less for hydro, bioenergy and wind.

This covers only units with a published fuel type and measures each instruction by the run of instructions that touch or overlap it; the other group is mostly, not wholly, batteries.

How I measured it
Chart
22 August 2025 to 30 September 2026, with the two halves of instructions that cross midnight joined.

Can the metered data be trusted?

How much do metered figures change when Elexon recalculates settlement with later meter data?

By a few per cent of the energy. In the week checked, 3.4% of the energy was revised between the one-week and three-week runs, and 2.3% (221 GWh of 9,494) between the three-week and two-month runs.

One week only. Over three-quarters of the later revision is in suppliers' customer accounts, which the other answers mostly leave out; three gas units carry most of the rest.

Limitations

  • Fuel groups are the registry's at each read.
  • The metered figure is the latest settlement run held at each read, which differs by date.
  • The plan (PN) is the latest published Final Physical Notification captured after gate closure.
  • Services NESO buys outside the balancing mechanism are not in the instructions (BOALF).
  • The units studied are not the whole grid: wind and solar on local networks outside the Balancing Mechanism are not in them, and batteries with no published fuel type are counted only in settlement revisions* and what is turned up with wind turned down*.
  • Results that set one measure against another (published outages*, demand or under-delivery*, carbon misses and under-delivery*, wind's shortfall*, wind and carbon misses* and what is turned up with wind turned down*) are associations, not causes. Each result covers the period given beside it and may not hold in another.
  • The forecast's drift* and accuracy by lead time* cover one window, 19 August to 12 November 2026, with no winter or spring. Inside it the accuracy result's bias changes sign: negative on its 365 earliest half-hours (19 August to 4 September 2026), positive on the 2,499 from 11 September. The drift's upward direction also holds on the 2,530 half-hours after 10 September 2026, 05:30 UTC; the accuracy figures point to a reversal on the earliest half-hours, for which the drift's own output has no figure. The split falls across a week-long gap in the sample as well as the change in the archive's poll schedule on 10 September 2026, and the saved outputs cannot tell a seasonal change from a capture effect.
  • The results cannot be rebuilt from public data: the archived forecast versions behind them cannot be fetched again from the public APIs, so only GridSkew's own copies hold them; superseded settlement runs cannot be fetched again either and are not copied.
  • The carbon API's half-hours may not line up with Elexon's. Four results (demand or under-delivery*, carbon misses and under-delivery*, wind's shortfall* and wind and carbon misses*) pair each carbon value with the Elexon half-hour of the same label, and misses by month and time of day* sets the API's actual against its forecast at the same label, as the API publishes them; accuracy by lead time* sets it against the archive's forecasts at the same label. Compared with the carbon intensity in NESO's historic generation mix, whose wind figures equal Elexon's at the same half-hour in 99.1% of the 17,280 half-hours from 22 August 2025 to 16 August 2026, the API's actual agrees most closely one half-hour after its own label: within 5 g/kWh in 45.8% of half-hours, against 35.8% at the same label; exactly equal in 5.9%, against 3.6%; a mean absolute difference of 15.3 g/kWh, against 16.8; and a correlation of half-hour changes of 0.61, against 0.43 (0.81 against 0.58 in GMT, 0.52 against 0.37 in BST). The share within 5 g/kWh and the mean absolute difference are best one half-hour after the label in all 13 months. This is a measured alignment, not a documented convention: the API labels each value with a half-hour but does not document whether its estimate is made for that half-hour or another, and how its forecast lines up is not settled. The results above stand as read; I make no claim about how the alignment bears on them. Alignment counts
  • GridSkew's data hold one impossible carbon actual, 0 g/kWh for 6 August 2026, 11:00 UTC. It lies inside the window of five results (demand or under-delivery*, carbon misses and under-delivery*, wind's shortfall*, misses by month and time of day* and wind and carbon misses*), in the August 2026 cell of the misses-by-month result.
How the questions were set up

Every method was recorded before its result was read, except where the result says otherwise; the exceptions are listed below, and where the data had been explored first, or a result is exploratory, the details say so. Each result was read once (the count of short instructions* was re-run once over the same period, with the two halves of instructions that cross midnight joined). "How I measured it", under each answer, gives the method, the numbers and the dates. A figure given for scale sets a result against a context count read on 1 November 2026, a denominator, not a result: planned energy, planned share of capacity, national demand and carbon intensity.

The 90% ranges are a guide to uncertainty, not part of any method's answer.
