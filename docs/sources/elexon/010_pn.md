# PN, Physical Notifications

**Ingested.** `raw.elexon_pn`, loaded daily by `gridskew_elexon_pn`. Schedule,
chunking and backfill are in [011_pn_ingestion.md](011_pn_ingestion.md).

## In plain terms

**The promise.** Ahead of time, every power station tells the grid operator how
much electricity it intends to produce in each half hour.

It is submitted as points on a line rather than a single number, so a station
ramping up says "I will be at 100 MW at 14:00 and 300 MW at 14:30" rather than
"I will average 200 MW".

Paired with `B1610`, which records what actually came out, this is how you find
generators that fell short of what they said they would do. That shortfall is
the starting point of the whole project.

> **`PN` may not be the whole promise.** `QPN` declares MW deducted from it to
> give the level the unit's Dynamic Data apply to. Whether that deduction also
> belongs in a shortfall calculation is **an open question** — the BSC glossary
> states QPN is not used in Settlement, while `B1610` is settlement metered
> volume. Resolve it against data before S8. See [015_qpn.md](015_qpn.md).

---

A Physical Notification is a participant's best estimate of what a BM unit will
export or import in a settlement period. It is the commitment side of the
thesis: what a generator said it would do, against which `B1610` records what it
actually did.

## Endpoint

```
GET https://data.elexon.co.uk/bmrs/api/v1/datasets/PN/stream
```

| Parameter | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `from` | yes | ISO datetime |
| `to` | yes | ISO datetime |
| `settlementPeriodFrom` | no | Untested on this dataset. On BOALF and MELS these switch `from`/`to` to filter on settlement **date** rather than time; on B1610 they had no effect. Verify before using |
| `settlementPeriodTo` | no | As above |
| `bmUnit` | no | Array. Not used in ingestion; pull everything |

No documented maximum range. Requests up to 18 days returned linear row counts
with no observed truncation. That establishes the tested depth, not an unlimited
range; ingestion uses daily chunks and does not depend on a larger window. See
[011_pn_ingestion.md](011_pn_ingestion.md).

## Only about an hour of the future is visible

Gate Closure is one hour before a settlement period begins, and Final Physical
Notifications must be submitted by then. Measured 2026-08-22 at 13:40 UTC:

| Window requested | Result |
|---|---|
| earlier today | data |
| 14:00 to 16:00 | data, ending 15:00 |
| 20:00 to 21:00 | empty |
| tomorrow, and beyond | empty |

So PN is **barely forward-looking**. It is not a day-ahead schedule; units file
close to the deadline and the horizon runs about eighty minutes out.

**Anything fetched for a period that has not passed Gate Closure is
provisional** and may still change. Fetching only up to the end of yesterday
avoids the question entirely.

## Response

Bare array, no envelope.

```json
[
  {
    "dataset": "PN",
    "settlementDate": "2026-08-01",
    "settlementPeriod": 5,
    "timeFrom": "2026-08-01T01:00:00Z",
    "timeTo": "2026-08-01T01:30:00Z",
    "levelFrom": 0,
    "levelTo": 0,
    "nationalGridBmUnit": "DRAXX-1",
    "bmUnit": "T_DRAXX-1"
  }
]
```

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `dataset` | `str` | Always `PN` |
| `settlementDate` | `str` | **Local time date.** See the source README |
| `settlementPeriod` | `int` | 1 to 50 |
| `timeFrom` | `str` | UTC, seconds, `Z` suffix |
| `timeTo` | `str` | UTC |
| `levelFrom` | `int` | MW at `timeFrom` |
| `levelTo` | `int` | MW at `timeTo` |
| `nationalGridBmUnit` | `str` | e.g. `DRAXX-1` |
| `bmUnit` | `str or null` | e.g. `T_DRAXX-1`. The Elexon identifier; null on 1.4% of the completed backfill |

## PN is a ramp, not a level

`levelFrom` and `levelTo` with `timeFrom` and `timeTo` describe a **straight
line between two points**, not a flat value for the period.

When output is steady, `levelFrom` equals `levelTo` and there is one row per
settlement period. When it changes, a unit submits several point pairs within a
single period and you get several rows.

### Confirmed live, 2026-08-21 — and it matters more than expected

Drax starting up, captured in
`tests/fixtures/elexon/pn_stream_ramp.json`:

| SP | Window | Minutes | MW | MWh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | 13:00–13:01 | **1** | 0 → 100 | 0.83 |
| 29 | 13:01–13:30 | 29 | 100 → 106 | 49.78 |
| 30 | 13:30–13:41 | 11 | 106 → 108 | 19.62 |
| 30 | 13:41–14:00 | 19 | 108 → 250 | 56.68 |
| 31 | 14:00–14:30 | 30 | 250 → 474 | 181.00 |

A **one-minute segment**, and two segments in each of two consecutive periods.
Observed again the following day on the same unit, so sub-period segments are
common rather than exceptional.

### The cost of getting the integration wrong

Settlement period 29 above, computed three ways:

| Method | MWh | Error |
|---|---|---|
| **Correct** — sum both segments | **50.62** | — |
| Period endpoints only | 26.50 | **−47.6%** |
| `level_to × 0.5h` | 53.00 | +4.7% |

**Nearly half the energy vanishes** under the plausible-looking approach of
taking the period's first and last levels and drawing one line between them.

B1610 reports metered volume in MWh for a whole settlement period; PN reports MW
at instants. Comparing them means integrating the ramp segment by segment. **That
integration is load-bearing in the planned commitment fact** — without it, the
shortfall figure for any ramping unit is wrong by tens of percent, and ramping
units are exactly what the thesis is about.

## `bmUnit` is null, and increasingly so

**Counted across a full year**, 46,190,258 rows to 2026-08-23:

| | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Null `bmUnit` | **657,743** | 1.4% |
| Null `nationalGridBmUnit` | **0** | — |
| Both null | **0** | — |

Units such as `I2G-POWT1` and `BLYDB-1` publish notifications with a National
Grid identifier and no Elexon one. This is the same phenomenon the registry shows
at `ACHYW-1` — a unit known to National Grid without an Elexon registration.

**So `nationalGridBmUnit` is the reliable identifier, not `bmUnit`.** Any join or
filter written on `bm_unit` silently drops those units.

**The rate is rising, not stable.** The yearly mean is 1,797 null rows per run,
but 2026-08-22 measured 2,450 — about 36% above the mean. Units are losing or
never acquiring an Elexon identifier over time, so this is a trend rather than a
fixed property, and the case for the National Grid key strengthens with age.

`raw.elexon_qpn` carries **exactly 657,743 null `bm_unit` rows too** — the same
number to the row. The same units are affected in both datasets, which turns the
key choice from an inference into a measurement.

A null `bmUnit` looks incoherent — a notification with no submitter — which is
why it is worth stating plainly that it is normal and expected here.

## Composition of the table

Counted across the same year:

| | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Zero to zero | 32,492,793 | **70.3%** |
| Ramps, `level_from <> level_to` | 3,525,386 | **7.6%** |
| Negative levels | 5,508,814 | **11.9%** |

**Negative levels are common, not exceptional.** Nearly one row in eight declares
an import — storage charging, interconnectors, demand units. Any check constraint
or downstream assumption requiring a non-negative level is wrong on 5.5M rows.

**7.6% are ramps**, which is the figure the settlement period integration has to
justify itself against. See "The cost of getting the integration wrong" above.

## Most rows are zero

- **70.3%** are zero-to-zero across the year
- On a single sampled day, **63% of units** (1,581 of 2,513) were zero throughout

The zeros are concentrated in dormant units rather than spread across active
ones. **Do not filter them at ingest.** A declared zero is a commitment, absence
would become ambiguous, and roughly 20,000 of those zero rows belong to units
that are active elsewhere in the same day — including the case where a unit
commits nothing and then delivers, which is what `BOALF` explains.

Filtering zeros would also break segment contiguity: a unit ramping down to zero
would keep its `(100 → 0)` segment and lose the `(0 → 0)` one after it, leaving a
period whose segments no longer tile it.

The right filter is a **registry join at S8**, scoping to physical generators,
which is reversible and time-varying in a way a row filter is not.

## Table

Built as `raw.elexon_pn` in `sql/init/003_elexon_pn.sql`:

```sql
PRIMARY KEY (national_grid_bm_unit, time_from, retrieved_at)
```

`bm_unit` is **nullable** and not part of the key, for the reason above.

`settlement_date` and `settlement_period` are columns but not key parts. Both are
derived from `time_from`, which is an absolute instant, so including them adds
index bytes without adding a guarantee.
